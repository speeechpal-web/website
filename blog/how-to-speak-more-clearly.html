How to speak more clearly

August 25, 2026 6 min read

If people ask you to repeat yourself, the problem usually isn’t volume. It’s articulation: the jaw barely opens, endings disappear, and the rush of the thought outruns the mouth.

Learning how to speak more clearly is a muscle skill. Short daily drills beat a vague promise to “enunciate more.”

Why speech gets muddy

Most unclear talking is a habit, not a personality. A tight jaw compresses vowels. Speed merges consonants. Word endings — the t, d, k — get swallowed because the breath drops before the sentence finishes. You hear the words you meant. Everyone else hears the ones you actually said.

Four drills that transfer to real talks

Practice these out loud for a few minutes. Then say one real sentence the same way.

  • Open the jaw. Read a paragraph with slightly bigger mouth movement. Clarity needs space.
  • Land the ending. Practice words like fact, kept, most, asked until the last sound is audible.
  • Slow the first three words. Rushing starts at the start. Set the pace, then continue.
  • Record 30 seconds. Explain something you know. Listen once. Fix one thing. Repeat.

Speak clearly under pressure

Interviews, meetings, and presentations are where mumbling returns. That’s why drills should look like the moment: a short answer, a point in a meeting, the first line of a talk. Challenge-specific practice beats generic tongue twisters you never use in the room.

Keep the loop small

One target a day. One recorded take. One correction. That’s how you talk more clearly without sounding fake or over-enunciated. SpeechPal’s articulation coach is built for that loop — drills, feedback, and reps that show up when it counts.
